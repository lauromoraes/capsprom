\chapter{Results}\label{chap:results}

\section{Dataset Validation Results}\label{sec:results-validation}

This section will report the final validation status of the model-ready
unambiguous-DNA datasets and summarize the effect of cleaning ambiguous symbols.
The completed Bhandari human audit found 162,863 duplicate training rows and
25,703 duplicate test rows in the legacy processed split. A total of 49,060 test
rows, corresponding to 49.87\% of that partition, belonged to exact sequences
also present in training. No identical sequence carried conflicting labels.
Consequently, metrics obtained from the legacy split are retained only as
development and provenance results.

The corrective pipeline produced 33,925 unique strict-DNA promoter windows and
the same number of deterministic mononucleotide shuffle controls. Its 54,280
training rows and 13,570 test rows contain neither internal duplicates nor exact
train--test overlap.

The same audit was then applied to the 26 CDBProm, CNNProm, and GraphPro pairs
that include source-provided non-promoters. Table~\ref{tab:results-real-negative-integrity}
summarizes the outcome by source. No pair passed the strict zero-duplicate and
zero-overlap criterion. Across the three sources, 129,739 of 132,272 test rows
(98.09\%) belong to exact sequences present in training. Four pairs also contain
label conflicts, dominated by 1,345 conflicting sequences in the GraphPro
\textit{E. coli} pair.

Provenance tracing showed that the legacy cropping notebook opened its outputs
in append mode. Repeated executions therefore wrote each promoter three times
and each non-promoter four times before row-level partitioning. After changing
the output to overwrite mode and regenerating all 26 pairs, test-row overlap
fell from 98.09\% to 2.17\%, internal duplicate occurrences fell from 379,046
to 1,614, and 9 pairs satisfied the strict integrity criterion without further
correction. The 1,348 unique label conflicts were unchanged, demonstrating that
they are independent of the append defect.

The subsequent mandatory collision filter removed every post-cropping sequence
observed with both labels from both classes. It removed 1,348 positive and 1,348
negative rows, reducing the collection from 183,701 cropped rows to 181,005
rows. Within-class deduplication then removed 301 positive and 759 negative
rows, and the strict-DNA filter removed a further six positive and two negative
rows. The resulting 179,937 unique strict-DNA sequences were assigned
deterministically to train or test as indivisible exact-sequence groups. The
canonical v3 audit contained zero internal duplicates, zero label conflicts,
and zero train--test overlap in all 26 pairs. Its deterministic audit identifier
is \texttt{87371a88ca1c5456db340201de5c19558b1a5773cb441787ffffc0803b66d853}.

Although every canonical dataset is internally group-safe, a separate audit
found exact identities shared across dataset boundaries. Table~\ref{tab:results-cross-dataset-dependency}
reports the eight affected pairs. These overlaps do not reintroduce within-
dataset train--test leakage, but they prevent all 26 dataset identities from
being assumed independent in cross-dataset statistical tests. Particularly,
the two \textit{B. amyloliquefaciens} sets share 535 sequences with opposing
labels, and CDBProm \textit{C. pneumoniae} shares 195 positive sequences with
the legacy GraphPro \texttt{Cpne} identity. Header review established that this
folder is also \textit{C. pneumoniae}, not \textit{C. phytofermentans}. The
reviewed confirmatory clusters therefore exclude GraphPro and are frozen over
the 18 eligible CDBProm and CNNProm identities.

The cross-source Bacillus finding further supports exclusion rather than
relabeling. Of the 928 raw GraphPro negative windows, 586 occur inside CDBProm
positive 1,001-bp regions and 535 begin at offset 440, exactly the canonical
start for a window from 60 bp upstream to 20 bp downstream of the CDBProm TSS at
position 500. Thus, the 535 opposite-label identities pre-exist as a raw
containment relation; cropping only reveals their exact equality.

The finalized confirmatory dataset contract consequently retains 18 datasets:
11 from CDBProm and seven from CNNProm. They form 14 dependency clusters after
shared source pools and exact cross-dataset overlaps are accounted for. All
eight GraphPro datasets remain available for exploratory reproduction and data
quality analysis, but do not contribute to confirmatory model ranking.

Independent reconstruction was initially evaluated for six nominal bacterial
identities. The official Promotech resource contains BED6 TSS tables and
reference sequences, but subsequent primary-source auditing showed that these
metadata do not form matching biological identities for every entry.
Table~\ref{tab:results-graphpro-reconstruction-feasibility} reports a
controlled infrastructure exercise using
predeclared construction parameters: 81 bp strand-oriented windows, a 100 bp
known-TSS exclusion radius, and one same-strand genomic control per positive.
GC tolerances of one through four nucleotides per 81 bp were tested without
training a model. Four nucleotides ($4/81=0.04938$) was the smallest tolerance
that supplied controls for all six organisms and was therefore frozen.

Primary-source resolution identified the ECOLI failure precisely. The nominal
BED contains 278 rows on \texttt{NC\_000913.3}, but their converted positions,
strands, and locus annotations reproduce all 278 ordered rows of Supplementary
Dataset S1 from PMID 27748404. That study maps the pAA plasmid of
\textit{E. coli} O104:H4. All 278 published 51-nt windows were independently
recreated from \texttt{NC\_018666.1}, whereas zero BED rows declare that
accession. Consequently, the 248 nominal MG1655 pairs in
Table~\ref{tab:results-graphpro-reconstruction-feasibility} and all derived
controls, partitions, similarity results, distribution statistics, and
cross-dataset overlaps are invalid provenance artifacts. They cannot be
repaired by renaming because their sequences were extracted from the wrong
molecule.

Before this source error was known, the infrastructure exercise generated
11,903 positive--control pairs. It collapsed 30 repeated nominal E. coli and 74
repeated Leptospira TSS rows by coordinate and strand, excluded
one boundary-limited Chlamydophila TSS, and excluded seven ambiguous and one
boundary-limited Leptospira windows. Post-generation sequence auditing then
removed the complete pair for one strand-invariant repeated positive in
BACILLUS, 23 in CJEJUNI\_2, and 53 in RHODOBACTER\_1. The version-2 candidate
therefore contains 11,826 balanced pairs with no exact or reverse-complement
identity within or across classes. It establishes computational feasibility,
not source eligibility. Similarity-aware component splitting retained all
11,826 pairs, assigning 9,460 pairs (18,920 rows) to training and 2,366 pairs
(4,732 rows) to testing. Every partition remained exactly class-balanced. A
direct post-split audit found zero shared pair or component identifiers and zero
train--test sequence neighbors through strand-invariant Hamming distance eight;
a clean rebuild reproduced every partition byte-for-byte.

The subsequent distribution gate found residual class structure despite
pair-level GC compliance. Every bacterial pair satisfied the frozen maximum
difference of four GC bases, but five of six organisms retained a statistically
detectable aggregate shift after Holm correction; mean positive-minus-control
differences ranged from $-0.0200$ to $+0.0031$. Exact cross-dataset auditing in
both orientations found 404 reconstructed Chlamydophila positives shared with
CDBProm, two reconstructed Bacillus positives shared with CDBProm, and six
nominal reconstructed E.~coli positives matching CNNProm negatives. Those six
comparisons are now invalidated by the assembly error rather than interpreted
as annotation disagreement. The first two valid-organism findings require
shared dependency clusters if the candidates are admitted. No external labels
were changed or removed on the basis of these disagreements. Primary-source
sampling and manual release review remain required.

The November 2023 EPDnew archive resolved every Human and Mouse identifier.
The 2,339 Human identifiers equal the complete HsNC v001 collection on hg38 and
occur in both released class files. The Mouse files contain 2,576 distinct
identifiers from MmNC v001 on mm10: 1,116 occur in both released classes and
730 occur only in each class. No identifier is unresolved. This closes the
coordinate-identity gate but does not validate the released negative class.
Complete strand-aware recreation from hash-verified hg38/mm10
assemblies found that all 4,185 released positives occupy $[-80,1)$ relative
to their EPD TSS and all 4,185 released negatives occupy $[1000,1081)$. All
8,370 sequences and embedded file labels matched exactly, and an independent
rerun produced byte-equal ledgers and summaries. Thus the released construction
is fully reproducible, although the downstream class is not an EPD-verified
non-promoter condition. Integrity-only filtering of the released files removed eight positive
and five negative Human records and three positive and one negative Mouse
records.

Table~\ref{tab:results-epd-downstream-treatment} reports the subsequent
strand-invariant and TSS-proximity treatment. One Human positive was a
within-class reverse-complement duplicate. Among controls surviving the earlier
integrity stage, 73 Human and 40 Mouse windows fell within the frozen 100-nt
margin of another coding or non-coding EPDnew TSS; 21 and 11, respectively,
contained that TSS directly.

The final populations contain no exact or reverse-complement duplicate within
class, no strand-invariant cross-label collision, and no remaining TSS-margin
violation. Their class-count differences are 69 Human and 38 Mouse records.
The retained released classes before this TSS treatment differ by 0.1096 and
0.1038 in mean GC fraction, respectively. After treatment, the corresponding
positive-minus-negative differences are 0.1149 for Human and 0.1069 for Mouse.
Two-sided Kolmogorov--Smirnov comparisons remain different after Holm
correction ($p=4.37\times10^{-99}$ and $p=1.55\times10^{-92}$), and the largest
positional total-variation distances are 0.397 at position $+20$ and 0.352 at
position $+19$, respectively. These are descriptive distribution diagnostics,
not validation of either class or criteria selected from model performance.

The strand-invariant dependency audit found one positive identity shared by
the treated Human and Mouse candidates. It also found that the Mouse control
\texttt{FP002834|Gm50063\_1} is the reverse complement of a CNNProm Mouse
non-TATA positive. Version 2 applied the predeclared rule that a genomic control
cannot retain an identity labeled positive in the active external comparison
scope. Only that GraphPro control was removed; CNNProm was neither modified nor
relabeled. Mouse therefore changed from 1,843/1,805 to 1,843/1,804, while Human
remained byte-identical at 2,330/2,261. Repeated auditing found no remaining
opposite-label dependency with the 18 confirmatory datasets, although the
same-label Human--Mouse positive dependency remains. Both historical downstream
conditions remain exploratory because their residual GC structure precludes
their use as the primary control condition. Neither candidate received a
grouped split or an execution gate.

The separate primary GC-matched reconstruction used the union of 2,339 Human
and 2,576 Mouse EPD identifiers. Exact, one-base, and random-only two-base
matching attempts were retained as distinct feasibility records. The final
version kept the two-base tolerance and added a deterministic exhaustive scan
only after 100,000 random probes were exhausted. It produced 2,332 Human pairs
after removing seven duplicate-positive pairs and 2,571 Mouse pairs after
removing five. Two extreme-GC Mouse promoters required exhaustive search:
\texttt{0610009L18Rik\_1} (75/81 G/C) and \texttt{Gm15327\_1} (76/81).

The mean positive-minus-control GC-fraction differences were 0.00289 for Human
and 0.00405 for Mouse. Two-sided Mann--Whitney and Kolmogorov--Smirnov tests did
not indicate an aggregate GC-distribution difference after Holm correction.
The maximum positional total-variation distance nevertheless remained 0.392
for Human and 0.411 for Mouse, both at the TSS. Exact dependency auditing found
only same-label positive overlap with CNNProm and one Human--Mouse positive;
there was no opposite-label identity. At strand-invariant Hamming distance
eight, 53 Human and 49 Mouse pairs belonged to non-singleton neighborhoods,
with largest components of six pairs. Human contained three cross-label
near-neighbor edges, while Mouse contained none. A clean rebuild reproduced the
complete output tree and execution log byte-for-byte. These results remove the
large univariate GC shortcut of the historical condition, but grouping, manual
source review, and eligibility approval remain required before any split or
training run.

Exact-identity correction does not exclude highly similar but non-identical
sequences. Table~\ref{tab:results-near-duplicate-sensitivity} therefore reports

sequences. The broadest threshold found 96 affected test sequences (0.267\%).
All 96 have at least one same-label training neighbor, while none has an
opposite-label neighbor within eight substitutions.

\subsection{Canonical source-negative fixed-seed sweep}

A frozen seed-42 sweep was completed on the 25 canonical v3 pairs whose test
outcomes were not observed during the smoke test. Table~\ref{tab:results-real-negative-fixed-seed}
summarizes the paired dataset-level MCC results. XGBoost produced a higher MCC
on 21 datasets, logistic regression on four, and no ties occurred. The median
paired difference was 0.0621 in favor of XGBoost, with a 95\% bootstrap interval
from 0.0322 to 0.0799 and a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$-value of
$1.23\times10^{-5}$. These fixed-seed values are preliminary because the
predeclared stochastic-model protocol requires ten XGBoost seeds before final
confirmatory inference.

\subsection{Canonical source-negative ten-seed replication}

in the earlier canonical-v3 scope. For each dataset, XGBoost was summarized by the median
MCC over seeds and paired with the single deterministic logistic-regression
result. Table~\ref{tab:results-real-negative-ten-seed} reports this exploratory
cross-dataset outcome. The median paired difference remained positive at 0.0574,
with a bootstrap 95\% interval from 0.0293 to 0.0777 and a two-sided Wilcoxon

21 datasets and logistic regression on four. The median within-dataset XGBoost

the direction of the fixed-seed screening result was retained after stochastic
replication.
