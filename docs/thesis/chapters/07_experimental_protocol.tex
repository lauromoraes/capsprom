\chapter{Experimental Protocol}\label{chap:experimental-protocol}

\section{Experiment Configuration}\label{sec:protocol-configuration}

Each experiment is specified by a tuple
$(\mathcal{D},\Phi,f,\theta,E,r)$, where $\mathcal{D}$ is the labeled dataset,
$\Phi$ is the combined feature representation, $f$ is the classifier or neural
architecture, $\theta$ is the set of training and model parameters, $E$ is the
set of evaluation outcomes, and $r$ denotes the random seed or replication
index. The configuration, dataset hashes, software environment, and resulting
artifacts are recorded through MLflow so that every reported value is traceable
to a versioned YAML specification.

The protocol distinguishes development from confirmatory evaluation. The
deduplicated Bhandari human shuffle-control split is the initial development
benchmark. Its negative examples are deterministic mononucleotide shuffles and
therefore test discrimination of promoter sequence order under controlled base
composition, not discrimination from genomic non-promoters. Because its test
results have already been observed during pipeline development, they are
reported as fixed-split reference results rather than as blind confirmatory
evidence. The contaminated legacy Bhandari partition is retained only for
provenance and historical comparability. Confirmatory comparisons use eligible
strict-DNA datasets from sources that provide non-promoter examples, including
CDBProm, CNNProm, and GraphPro, with model specifications and search spaces
frozen before test predictions are generated. Their legacy fixed partitions do
not satisfy the integrity criteria and are replaced by deduplicated group-safe
partitions before confirmatory use. Dataset-level results are the
primary units of cross-dataset inference, preventing the largest sources from
dominating the overall conclusion.

\section{Data Integrity and Model Selection}\label{sec:protocol-integrity}

Each train--test pair is audited for strict DNA compliance, class counts, exact
duplicates, conflicting labels, normalized partition overlap, and available
source groups. Immediately after cropping and before partitioning, every
normalized sequence observed with both labels is removed in full from the
positive and negative classes. No label is inferred or preferred. Repeated
same-label identities are collapsed to one row, strict-DNA filtering is applied,
and unique exact-sequence groups are assigned deterministically to train or
test. A filtering manifest records, for each dataset, the number of rows
entering, removed from, and remaining after cropping, collision removal,
within-class deduplication, strict-DNA filtering, and partitioning. Fixed
benchmark partitions are retained for comparability when
they pass the declared integrity criteria. A contaminated partition is retained
only as a labeled legacy analysis and is replaced by a group-safe split for the
primary development workflow, as in the Bhandari human correction. When
biological or source groups are available, resampling preserves those groups.

Eligibility is evaluated after all declared filters. A confirmatory pair must
contain both classes in both partitions, no same-label exact duplicate rows, no
cross-label collisions, and no exact sequence shared by training and test.
Failure of any condition blocks confirmatory use but does not erase the source
artifact: the original hashes, counts, reason for exclusion, correction policy,
and corrected hashes remain in the audit trail. Near-duplicate and homology
analyses are reported separately and do not replace these exact-identity gates.

All data-dependent preprocessing and feature selection are fitted only on the
training partition. Hyperparameters are selected using one fixed five-fold
stratified training manifest generated with seed 42. Models in a comparison
receive an explicitly comparable search budget. Test outcomes are not used for
feature selection, early stopping, threshold selection, calibration, or
hyperparameter optimization.

\section{Replication and Prediction Contract}\label{sec:protocol-replication}

Stochastic models are trained with ten predeclared seeds. The fixed seed set is
shown below so that it can be reproduced independently of the implementation:
\[
  \{13,29,42,71,101,137,173,211,251,293\}.
\]
Every stochastic component receives the run seed. Deterministic models are
fitted once per locked configuration and are not repeated to create
pseudo-replicates. Stochastic dataset-level results are summarized primarily by
their median and interquartile range, with mean and standard deviation retained
as supplementary descriptors.

Every test record receives a positive-class score and a binary prediction. The
primary decision threshold is 0.5. A threshold selected exclusively from
out-of-fold training predictions may be reported as a secondary analysis but is
frozen before test evaluation. Sigmoid or isotonic calibration is likewise fit
only from training-derived out-of-fold predictions; raw and calibrated models
are treated as separate locked conditions.

\section{Evaluation Outcomes}\label{sec:protocol-metrics}

Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) is the single primary effectiveness
outcome because it incorporates every confusion-matrix cell and remains
informative under class imbalance. Table~\ref{tab:protocol-outcomes} summarizes
the outcome families used to characterize effectiveness, calibration,
efficiency, and robustness. Secondary metric inference is interpreted as
exploratory so that multiple correlated measures do not inflate claims of
superiority.

Efficiency timing uses one untimed warm-up followed by three timed executions
under the same hardware and resource settings. Median and interquartile range
are reported together with peak resident memory, software versions, processor
or accelerator identity, thread count, and input size.

\section{Uncertainty and Statistical Comparison}\label{sec:protocol-statistics}

For each dataset and locked run, 95\% confidence intervals are estimated from
10,000 stratified bootstrap resamples of the test records. The same resampled
indices are applied to every model in a pair. A group bootstrap replaces
record-level resampling when defensible biological or source groups are
available; otherwise, the possible dependence between sequences is stated as a
limitation.

Within one dataset, the principal effect is the paired difference in MCC. The
effect estimate is accompanied by its paired-bootstrap interval and a two-sided
paired randomization test with 10,000 swaps. McNemar's exact test is a secondary
analysis of discordant classification errors. Across at least ten eligible
datasets, a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test evaluates dataset-level MCC
differences, accompanied by the median paired difference and win--tie--loss
counts.

The family-wise significance level is 0.05. Holm correction is applied across
confirmatory model-pair hypotheses for the primary outcome. Conclusions
emphasize effect sizes and uncertainty; statistical significance alone is not
interpreted as biological importance.

\section{Reproducibility and Reporting}\label{sec:protocol-controls}

Each MLflow run records the protocol version, dataset and fold-manifest hashes,
feature and model definitions, seed, software and hardware metadata, row-level
test predictions, effectiveness and calibration outcomes, timing and memory
measurements, resampling seeds, comparison outputs, and fitted artifacts.
Prediction files use stable row identifiers without unnecessarily reproducing
raw sequences.

Reports label analyses as exploratory, developmental, or confirmatory and state
dataset eligibility, exclusions, failed runs, replication counts, and the
resampling unit. The complete machine-readable contract is maintained in
\path{configs/evaluation_protocol.yaml}, while implementation requirements and
limitations are maintained in
\path{specs/statistical_evaluation_protocol.md}.
